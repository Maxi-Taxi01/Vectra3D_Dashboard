# Sponsor review: make the business plan fit available time

Prepared 10 September 2026 from the supplied proposal. Administrative arithmetic only; no market prices or achieved financial results are assumed.

## The decision
The main graduation plan caps commercial work at four hours per week outside forty graduation hours. Appendix A estimates 164 hours for a first standard project. Before promising a delivery date, reconcile those two statements with actual work already committed.

| Source assumption or calculation | Amount | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Commercial cap, Proposal p.6 | 4 h/week | Separate from graduation time |
| 19 working weeks × 4 h | 76 h | Full-period budget under a working-weeks-only interpretation, not hours remaining today |
| First project estimate, p.13 | 164 h | 41 weeks at 4 h/week, before any extra work outside that estimate |
| First-project deficit against 76 h | 88 h | A full project does not fit that budget |
| First and second project estimates | 164 + 100 = 264 h | 66 weeks at 4 h/week; does not include additional uncounted business work |
| Six-month comparison, assuming 26 weeks | 26 × 4 = 104 h | Still below the roughly 500 h stated in Appendix B, p.15 |

These comparisons do not assume holiday availability. Even allowing four commercial hours in every one of the 22 calendar weeks spanning the agreement period gives 88 hours, still less than 164. The proposal protects holidays, so the 88-hour number is a sensitivity check, not a schedule to adopt. Current remaining capacity is lower than full-period capacity and needs actual logged hours and commitments.

## Proposed basis for the sponsor conversation
Retain the graduation time boundary while establishing what can realistically be sold and delivered. A paid intake or tightly specified existing service may be possible, but its scope, actual required hours and margin are not yet established. Do not assume a smaller sale fits merely because its price is lower.

Consider these paths with the sponsor:
- Keep the cap and arrange full-project delivery after graduation, if a buyer accepts the timing.
- Accept only work whose complete remaining effort fits the available commercial hours, with a written scope and revision limit.
- If the plan truly depends on larger delivery capacity, seek the required plan approval before changing the allocation; do not quietly consume the 40 graduation hours or count the same hour twice.

No path has been approved or communicated to a customer by this document.

## Separate the three money measures
**Project contribution:** project revenue less its direct external costs. The proposal example is €8,950 − €750 = €8,200. At 164 own hours that is €50 per hour before fixed overhead and personal tax. This is a source-based estimate, not actual profit.

**Business result for a period:** earned revenue less direct costs and the period's overhead, using a consistent basis. Divide by all own business hours, including sales, preparation, revisions and administration, to see the return on time. Do not mix a six-month overhead total with a single week's revenue or omit unpaid hours.

**Cash available:** cash actually received less cash actually paid. An invoice, signed quote or expected payment is not cash received. The proposal's advance-payment policy and reserve target are plans until transactions verify them.

Max's stated floor is €1,000 monthly business income with growth over time (10 September). Whether that means revenue, business result before personal tax or personal take-home income is awaiting clarification. The dashboard cannot establish any of these results from the available evidence.

For capacity discussion only: four hours/week averaged over a full 52-week year is 17.33 hours/month. A €1,000 monthly result would require about €57.69 per available hour under that simplified full-utilisation model. If the target is profit, that return must remain after business costs; if it is take-home income, personal tax also matters. This is arithmetic, not a selling price, a forecast or a reason to increase the graduation time allocation. Actual monthly working days, billable share, overhead and holiday limits still need to be included.
